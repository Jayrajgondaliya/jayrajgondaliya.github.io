Are You Using AI Like Google Maps?

Using AI
May 4, 2026 · Jayraj Gondaliya

Are You Using AI Like Google Maps? (Spoiler: You’re Probably Lost)#

Here’s a confession:

I use Google Maps so much that I can’t remember street names in my own city.
I go from Point A to Point B like a well-trained pigeon.

The area between A and B?
No idea. Could be a park. Could be a secret taco 🌮 spot. Could be a portal to Narnia.

Who knows?, Is this bad?

Well…, Some people might say it saves time. Others say I’m missing hidden gems, weird statues, and that one random place everyone takes selfies with.

It’s like taking the highway from SF to LA. Sure, you get there fast.

But you miss: - tiny coastal towns - sketchy but amazing food stands - that scenic overlook where you question your life choices - or accidentally meet someone who changes your life (or at least your weekend)

So the real question is:

Do you want a fast destination… or an interesting journey?

But here’s the kicker:

What if there’s a massive crash on the highway?
What if Maps dies?

Will you know how to get home?

Or will you just… sit there… panicking… like a lost GPS satellite?

Welcome to the AI Highway Problem#

AI is everywhere.

Your IDE has it. Your browser has it.
At this point, your toaster is probably running a small LLM, or SLM (Small Language Model).

And If you use AI even a little bit, you’ve probably noticed something weird.

Your brain—this insanely powerful problem-solving machine—
now has a default response:

“lol just ask ChatGPT”

My personal shame:

If you’re a software engineer, be honest:

When was the last time you actually thought through a problem
before pasting it into AI?

Yeah.

Your problem-solving muscles?
They’re getting soft.

Should We Just Stop Using AI?#

Hell no.

That’s like saying:

“Google Maps made me lazy, so I’ll navigate using the stars like a medieval sailor.”

Relax, Vasco da Gama, not coming after you

The problem isn’t AI.

The problem is using AI like a vending machine.

Most People Use AI Wrong (Including Me)#

The typical approach:

You: "Give me a shoulder and back workout."

AI: [Generates routine]

You: Start Following

Congratulations.

You’ve successfully outsourced your brain.

You are now a biological copy-paste machine.

and you have used AI as a information rearranging tool.
