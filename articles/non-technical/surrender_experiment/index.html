Surrender Isn’t Weakness — This Book Proved It

February 2, 2026

Surrender. What does this word really mean, and why am I even talking about it? Most people associate surrender with weakness. But is surrender truly a weakness, or can it lead us to contentment, inner peace, and something greater?

Michael A. Singer wrote this book and shared his experiences with surrender. But surrender to what? It is surrendering to the will of the universe. It means accepting situations and circumstances as they are, without adding our personal preferences or trying to remove what we dislike, while staying detached from the outcome. Instead, surrender is about letting go of resistance and focusing on serving whatever life places in front of us, to the best of our ability.

For a simple example, imagine it is raining. You can just say, “It is raining,” instead of reacting with, “Oh damn, it is raining again,” or “Wow, it is raining.” The rain is simply the situation in front of you. I am not saying you should not enjoy the rain; I am saying you should not disturb your inner peace because of it.

But first, let me share my own experience with it. I first came across Michael A. Singer and his book The Untethered Soul through a Tony Robbins podcast. Even Oprah had spoken about it, so I decided I would read it someday. A few days later, while roaming around a gift shop in Los Gatos, California, the shop had everything from decoration items to incense sticks. In one corner, I noticed a few books. I randomly picked up one of them, noticed the author’s name, and decided to buy it. It was The Surrender Experiment.

I ended up buying this book instead of The Untethered Soul, and it became a “shelf-help” book rather than a self-help book. Fast forward two years, I suddenly picked up this and finished reading it.

I’m not sure why it took me two years to read this book. But if I had read it earlier, I might not have written this blog at all. I guess this, too, is the will of the universe.

Now, back to the book. I bet all of us experience something similar on a daily basis. If you look back, you might find multiple moments where something happened to you — or around you — that felt like magic or a kind of divine intervention, gently leading you somewhere else.

Become a member Maybe you were short $200 on rent and someone unexpectedly gave you a tip. Or you read something at the last minute before an exam, and that exact question appeared on the test, helping you score well.

I once heard about a woman who couldn’t reach her office on time because she got a splinter from the railing of her house while leaving for work. That small delay caused her to miss her train. Later that day, 9/11 happened — and that delay ended up saving her life.

When you think about it, there could be hundreds of such things happening around us without us even noticing, quietly working in our favor.

This might sound like hocus-pocus, but trust me — I’m a rational and analytical person. I’m not trying to convince anyone about God or spirituality. I’m just pointing out that when you look at life closely, there are some events you simply can’t explain.

I remember one incident from my life back in India. I had just started my career after undergrad and joined a small company. After three months, the owner didn’t pay me. When I confronted him, we had a heated argument, and he fired me without giving me my salary. I was very angry and frustrated about everything, and desperate to do something for my career.

Around that time, I came across a coaching center for IELTS and GRE. They were charging 20,000 rupees — the exact amount I had left in my bank account. Since I was angry with everyone (young blood — 😆), I paid the non-refundable fees. I was kinda stuck to go to coaching and finish what I had started. That decision led me to score well on the exams, get into top universities in the USA, and eventually work for large corporations.

Looking back after reading The Surrender Experiment, I realize that if this hadn’t happened, I might still have been stuck at that small company. It really feels like the will of the universe. I have many such incidents in my life, where things worked out in ways I couldn’t have planned.

This book is full of incidents like these from the author’s life. By surrendering to the will of the universe and focusing on serving whatever opportunities come, giving 100% of himself, Michael A. Singer went from living in his van in the woods to becoming the CEO of a multi-billion dollar company. He also wrote multiple books and accomplished many extraordinary things. I think we should read this with an open mind — it’s a good and fun read.

So, when things feel out of control, or when you give your 100% and the results don’t follow, remember to see it as the will of the universe. Don’t let it affect your efforts next time. Trust that something bigger and better is coming your way.

And my final message: Enjoy the ride we call life — seatbelts optional, fun mandatory.

— Jayraj Gondaliya
